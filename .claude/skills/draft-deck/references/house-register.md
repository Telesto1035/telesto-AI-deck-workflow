# House register: writing conventions carried across decks

Conventions for how a Telesto page is written, distilled from the edits consultants and the CEO made to AI drafts. Client-specific wording and formatting live in `clients/<client>.md`, not here.

## The standard this file holds itself to

**The target is general professional consulting writing, not a reconstruction of how a Telesto consultant sounds.** The bar is the register of an MBB or Big Four deliverable, or a memo to an F500 executive: correct, specific, claims sized to evidence, free of machine phrasing. Inside that bar there is wide latitude, and two consultants at the same firm write differently while both are right.

A convention earns a place here only if a reader outside the firm could apply it the same way the author would. Everything narrower is a preference, and preferences are collected at the bottom of this file, where a drafter may read them and no check may cite them.

**Nothing in this file is scored.** deck-qc dropped the register dimension: a 1 to 5 verdict on voice is a taste judgment wearing a number, it cannot be calibrated across people, and it cannot be enforced once the skills are shared. The conventions below produce findings that quote a rule, or they produce nothing.

## Governance
- **Cap: 25 enforceable conventions.** A new one above the cap retires one.
- **Entry: recurrence only.** A pattern enters as enforceable after the same edit is made by a human pen across two or more shipped decks, *and* it passes the outside-reader test above. The question the register answers is narrow: what language does a human consistently edit out or rephrase? A pattern that recurs but is a matter of taste enters Preferences instead.
- **Instances inside one deck do not corroborate.** One pass over one document is one observation however many times it repeats, and "7 instances" or "every page" is one consultant's preference counted seven times. Record instances if they are useful, but the deck count is what clears the bar.
- **A ruling is not an entry path.** It is one voice on one day, and the register is built collaboratively from what the work shows. A ruling can retire a rule, pause one, or settle which of two readings to try next; it cannot promote a pattern the decks have not shown twice.
- **Conflict:** two patterns that conflict are both held until the decks resolve which one recurs. The rule that loses moves to Retired with the date and what replaced it.
- **Single-deck observations wait**, firm-wide ones under "Observed, not yet promoted" below, client-specific ones in the client file under the same heading.
- **Refresh:** reviewed when Learn runs and at least twice a year; a rule no consultant edit has touched in a year is a candidate for retirement or for the principles file.
- **Format:** rule in bold, one example, then `[evidence: N decks, YYYY-MM]` where N counts decks. No narrative.

## Enforceable conventions (3)

A check may cite these. Each is decidable by reading the page against the rule, with no knowledge of the firm, and each rests on the same edit recurring across two shipped decks. A convention marked *(drafting craft)* shapes a draft and produces no review item, as in `language-rules.md`.

### Titles
1. **A title states the finding, not the method, the label or the survey structure.** "Secondary analysis of competitor articles reveals whitespace for [topic]" became "On competitor websites, [topic] is scarcely covered by any of the six firms". [evidence: 2 decks, 2026-09] *(drafting craft)* [owner ruling, 2026-09-23: the CEO-approved proposals title structural pages such as the options page by what the page holds]
2. **Interpretation leaves the title and goes to the band.** A title that ends ": the opening is [X], not the case for [Y]" loses the clause; the band carries the thought. [evidence: 2 decks, 2026-09] *(drafting craft)* [owner ruling, 2026-09-23]

### Claims and evidence
3. **Size the claim to the number; a ratio survives only when exact; a named firm as subject takes a softer verb.** "twice the prime-bid path" became "larger than the prime-bid path"; "has the least of it deployed" became "does not see it deployed actively". [evidence: 2 decks, 2026-09]

## Observed, not yet promoted (firm-wide)

Patterns seen in one deck, or resting on a ruling rather than on recurrence. They are held here until the same edit appears in a second shipped deck. **No check may cite them, and they produce no finding**: they are notes for the next Learn run, not rules. Demoted 2026-09-16 when the entry bar became recurrence only.

- No label-colon opener, no abstract-noun subject, no compressed verb-noun, no prescription in a title. "Adjacency quadrant: 35 markets" became "We ranked 35 service markets by size and closeness to the client's core". (1 deck, 5 titles, 2026-09-12; the label-prefix half is stated independently in `language-rules.md`.)
- Fewer names in a title: the two that matter, and where they matter. (1 deck, 2026-09-12)
- A band drops meta lines about the deck itself. "The wider report is where that association gets built" was cut. (1 deck, 2 passes, 2026-09-12)
- Figures from 10 upward, words below; fiscal-year ranges as "FY24-25". (1 deck, 7 instances, 2026-09-12)
- One source line per deck, with no internal counts and no repository or dashboard names. (1 deck, every page, 2026-09-12)

## Preferences, not enforced

House habits a drafter may follow and **no check may cite, score or class as a defect**. They are recorded because they are real preferences that have been expressed, and they sit outside the enforceable list because two competent consultants would disagree about them. A deck that ignores every line below is not wrong.

- Findings often read better framed as the client's opportunity, with the body ending on where the client stands. "Leaders rank [topic] second" became "Leaders need help with [topic]". [consultant ruling, 2026-09-12]
- A negative finding usually carries its opportunity on the same page under a neutral head: "Where the client already stands" and the number, rather than "the good news" or "the bad news". [consultant and owner rulings, 2026-09-12]
- Burning-platform framing is usually worth at most once per deck, sized to the evidence; offering the partner's strength and the data's, and letting the consultant choose, works well. [consultant ruling, 2026-09-12]
- Recommendations often land better naming the client's priority with the imperative in "should" or "needs" than in a bare command verb. This is a house habit and not a standard: MBB and Big Four decks use the command verb routinely. An evaluator that flagged a command verb as a defect was applying this line as a rule, which it is not. [2 decks; CEO-approved proposals, 2026-09]
- Bands usually bold the two or three phrases that carry the action.
- The skill owner edits out these words on their own work: earned, already, carry / carries, load-bearing, recast, hypothesis, "this confirms [client]". A drafter may follow it. It is not a standard and no check may cite it: these are ordinary English words, and a reader outside the firm could not apply the line the same way. [owner ruling, 2026-09-12; moved here 2026-09-16]
- Next steps often read as proposals the room decides on ("Discuss and select"), leaving the naming of themes to the room.
