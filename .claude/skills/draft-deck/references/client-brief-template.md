# Client brief template

One file per core client: the context file for that client's Cowork project, or a file in its engagement folder. Never in this repo, and never in another client's project. The skills read it at the start of every session in the project, or when it is attached. It is context, not evidence: no figure in it reaches a slide unless the deck's own sources carry it.

Two refresh triggers, both by a consultant: quarterly, after the earnings release (results, guidance, acquisitions, leadership, sections 1 to 4); and after any client meeting, section 7. Section 5 is refreshed when a new shipped deck changes the client's style.

Copy from the line below. About three pages.

**Three rules for what the team adds**
1. **Names are fine on a slide where the deck calls for them:** an introductions page, a question put to a named person, a next-step owner. An owner may equally be a team or department.
2. **Opinions about people never go on a slide:** how someone is seen, what they privately want, their standing or performance. Better still, leave them out of this file; if one has to be written down, it goes in section 8, marked internal.
3. **Section 7 records what the client said,** in their words, with the date and the speaker's role. It is context for the recommendation and is not quoted on a slide as written.

## 1. The company in one paragraph
What it sells, to whom, how it reports revenue, current-year guidance, headcount, stated medium-term goals.

## 2. Strengths and weaknesses, from the public record
Four of each, one line apiece, each traceable to a source in section 9. The first weakness is the one Telesto's work addresses.

## 3. Key departments and services
Segments or business units with the services each sells and named leaders where public; cross-cutting capabilities; corporate officers; where marketing sits.

## 4. Acquisitions and divestitures, last 12 months
Date, target, segment, what it adds.

## 5. Deck style
From the client's shipped decks, named with their type and month. Paper (template, fonts, color roles; colors are read from the attached deck or a palette screenshot, never listed as hex codes), recurring pages and elements, and voice (client vocabulary, title conventions, how far claims go). A freshly attached shipped deck wins over this section. Mark what the decks do not settle `[TO CONFIRM]`.

## 6. Key account stakeholders
The people and teams Telesto works with: name, role, where it sits, three or four responsibilities from job postings, public pages or the engagement. Facts only; see the three rules above.

## 7. Stated objectives (Telesto's record; update after meetings)
Goals and challenges in the client's words: competitor to beat, perception to shift, line to grow, market to enter, what they hired Telesto to find out. Then a dated log:

| Date | Meeting | Who (role) | What they said | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | | | open / done / superseded |

## 8. Additional context
Anything else the team knows: how the client likes to receive work (PPTX, PDF, pre-read timing, whether presenters use speaker notes), a brand guide, prior Telesto work, decision process.

## 9. Sources
Filing, release, page or posting, with URL.
