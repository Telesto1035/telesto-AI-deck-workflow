# Deck types

Eight types. Each lists the elements that usually appear, the length such decks usually run, and a register. None of this is a fixed order or a limit: the storyline decides the order, the content decides the length (a minipack can run past 11 pages when the client should see it), and the consultant confirms both in text before any build. The gate reports length as information only, and the `--length` option passes a variant's range, such as `6-12` for a findings deck whose detail sits in a product. Paper (Telesto or client) sets colors, fonts and furniture only; density follows the type and the page's job.

## Findings / survey report
The deck a room of MDs, team leads and directors reads for an hour. No ambiguity, no AI-speak, no unexplained number.
- **Usually present:** cover with the takeaway as title; executive summary with numbered findings, each a bold finding sentence with the key numbers inline and a labelled implication; methodology with n and margin of error, stating which cuts are directional; one or two pages per finding (numbered eyebrow, full-sentence action title, one or two exhibits, recommendation band, source and n under every exhibit, question wording verbatim in the footnote); closing recommendations or next steps; appendix after a divider, not counted in length.
- **Exhibits:** native, editable charts from the data files. Bars and heatmaps for many percentages; paired bars for two groups; stacked scales for rating batteries; quote pages with verbatim attributed quotes.
- **Length:** 10 to 25 pages usually; 6 to 12 when a product holds the detail.
- **Register:** findings declarative and data-anchored; implications labelled and separate; bands in the should-register.

## Proposal / BD
Speaks the buyer's language: why now, what problem, what the client gains, what the buyer decides.
- **Usually present:** cover with the takeaway as title and "Prepared for"; the stakes or where we left off (stat rail; what the client stands to gain, in dollars only where the consultant supplies a figure, and then labelled illustrative with assumptions); the problem and why now; options in tiers with the recommended tier marked and an outcome line each; path and first 30 days; team and terms where asked. Numbered eyebrows carry the spine (01 THE STAKES, 02 THE OPTIONS, and so on).
- **Page grammar:** visual throughout: stat callouts, option cards, chevrons, timeline, closing band. A chart only where one number needs proof.
- **Length:** 4 to 7 pages.
- **Register:** outcome-led; credit the client's own direction and let their criteria select the answer. Fees, durations and value-at-stake inputs come from the consultant or become `[PLACEHOLDER: builder input needed]`.
- **Exemplars:** the two CEO-approved proposals of Aug 2026 (7 and 4 pages) are the style guide for how a proposal recommends.

## Minipack
The between-milestones deck that answers one question with evidence. Its shape is stable so a reader who has seen one can read the next without orientation; the order can still bend to the storyline.
- **Usually present:** cover with the topic and the stake as title; callout page with the one-sentence takeaway in large type; executive summary with four numbered findings written as the client's opportunity, each with a one-line implication; one or two detail pages per finding sharing the eyebrow number; next steps with owners and dates. No conclusion page: the bands and the next steps close the argument. Appendix after a divider.
- **Page patterns:** stat column beside quote cards; two ranked bar charts side by side; a small-multiple matrix of attributes by segment; a quotes page in two themed groups; a readiness table with a key-takeaways panel.
- **Length:** 8 to 11 body pages usually.
- **Register:** findings as the client's opportunity; bands bold the action phrase and carry the client proof the consultant supplies.

## Kickoff, Presentation, Update, Workshop
- **Kickoff:** objectives and success, scope and workstreams, approach and timeline, roles and cadence, first two weeks. 5 to 8 pages; operational register with dates and owners.
- **Presentation:** a findings or proposal deck with fewer words and larger exhibits, closing on a decision page. 8 to 15 pages. Explainer variant for a product: problem, what it does, how the client uses it, value, what happens next, 3 to 5 pages.
- **Update:** where we are against plan, what we learned, decisions needed, next period. 3 to 6 pages; plain, dated, owner-named.
- **Workshop:** agenda and objectives, framing pages, exercise pages with prompts, empty capture grids, close. 6 to 12 pages; questions and prompts, not conclusions.

## Manual / user guide
Teaches a reader to operate a product or a workflow unassisted. It does not pitch and does not build a recommendation.
- **Usually present:** cover; what this replaces and why; orientation (the parts and how they relate); a divider per part; one page per operation stating what the reader does, what comes back and what the reader decides; demonstration pages; a one-sentence takeaway band on most pages.
- **Storyline test:** coverage, not argument: every operation the reader performs has a page.
- **Length:** 15 to 25 pages.
- **Register:** plain instruction to the operator. Any gain claimed (time, iterations, cost) carries the measure that proves it, or is not claimed.

## Page patterns that recur across types
Content structure only; colors, fonts and furniture come from the attached deck.
- **Exec summary, numbered finding plus implication:** 4 to 6 items; item numbers carry through the deck as eyebrows on the detail pages.
- **Chart plus takeaway rail:** main chart about two thirds width, two to four quantified takeaways beside it; chart subtitle carries metric and denominator.
- **Dual-chart comparison:** two related questions side by side, each with its own denominator and footnote; a short callout strip quantifies the difference.
- **Quotes page:** title states the base ("from 89 free responses"); quotes verbatim, attributed by role and segment, grouped under small topical headers; never edit quote wording.
- **Segment split:** two populations across three or four dimensions in paired mini-charts; legend states each group's n; callouts quantify the gap.
- **Self-rating stack:** question text left, stacked-bar distribution right with collapsed scale points; light on interpretation.
- **Coverage or whitespace matrix:** rows are topics, columns are sources or segments, cells carry intensity with parenthetical examples; a bottom row calls out whitespace; footnote states corpus and window.
- **Methodology:** who, n, how sampled, when; margin of error; which cuts are directional and their n.
- **Profile series:** one layout repeated per subject; build one, confirm it, then replicate.
