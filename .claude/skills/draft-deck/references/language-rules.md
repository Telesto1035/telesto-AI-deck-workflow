# Language rules

House style for titles, takeaways, and slide text. Applies on both papers (thinking + craft always apply; only branding changes by paper). Examples are drawn from shipped decks.

## Action titles

The title IS the takeaway. A reader skimming only titles gets the full argument.

- Full sentence, verb-led, specific, ~8–20 words.
- Good: "Buyers select based on peer referrals, higher-ed experience, and personalized proposals, rather than cost."
- Good: "When forced to prioritize, leaders rank financial sustainability and AI as top challenges, while deprioritizing public trust and student preparedness."
- Bad: "Selection factors" (label, not takeaway). Bad: "Huron dominates the market" (over-claim).
- No label prefix on a title. "Where to play:", "Who to target |" and "What to say |" were removed from every title on the Sep 2026 public sector deck before it shipped; the storyline position is carried by the eyebrow or the section divider, and the title is the assertion alone. The older series-prefix pattern (`Mainstream | …`) is retired.
- Two-part titles (title + subtitle continuation, "…despite top challenges' lack of resource and leadership attention") are allowed sparingly for tension between two facts.

## Sizing claims to evidence

- **No superlatives, no vague intensifiers.** Banned unless quoting: significant(ly), massive, dramatic, clear winner, game-changing, unprecedented, extremely (outside scale labels). If it matters, give the number.
- **No "every" or "never" claims from single-survey data.** "Outperform every paid channel" becomes "outperform paid outreach channels". Totalizing words survive only when the data is literally total.
- **Quantify deltas**: "26pts difference", "2X the rate", "80% vs. 48%" — never "far more" alone. ("far more than the C-suite (26pts difference)" is fine: adjective + number.)
- **Verbs scale with evidence.** One data point: "indicates", "points to", "suggests". Converging data points: "shows", "finds". Reserve "confirms"/"proves" for near-tautologies, and prefer not to use them.
- **Subgroup findings stay subgroup findings.** "Tech leaders elevate cybersecurity sharply" — do not promote to "leaders elevate cybersecurity".
- **Small n:** label cuts "directional only"; state exclusions ("Chartis and CCS have low end counts (n<18); … excluded (n<5)"). State margin of error in methodology.
- **Tension is stated, not hidden**: "even though a larger share say partners' AI tools justify their fees" — keep the countervailing number next to the headline number.
- **No legal or regulatory judgments** the dataset cannot support. Describe what was observed; do not conclude compliance or violation.

## Provenance — every number has a home

- Chart subtitle convention: `% of respondents selecting attribute · n=250` (metric · denominator).
- Question sourcing in footnotes: `Q12: Which of the following cost reduction strategies…`. Cross-survey borrowings named: "From Competitive Intelligence survey: …".
- Quotes verbatim, in quotation marks, with attribution: `"…" (Board of trustees, R1)`.
- Missing number → `[PLACEHOLDER: source needed]`. Never estimate and present as fact. Never reuse a number from a different base without re-stating the base.

## Finding vs. implication

Two registers, kept separate:
- **Finding**: declarative, data-anchored. "AI is changing what buyers will pay for, posing material risk to 2027 revenue. 68% agree consultant fees should be lower if the firm is using AI tools…"
- **Implication**: imperative, action-anchored, labeled. "Implication: Position Huron's digital modernization as building a safe foundation for future AI deployment…"

A finding never smuggles in a recommendation; an implication never introduces new data.

## Craft

- Active verbs. "Leaders rank financial sustainability highest", not "Financial sustainability was ranked highest by leaders".
- Verbs stay verbs: "decide", not "make a decision"; "prioritize", not "conduct prioritization".
- Short sentences. Split any sentence carrying more than one "that/which/as well as".
- No filler, no empty transitions, no consulting hollow-speak (force multiplier, boil the ocean, synergies).
- No AI-sounding constructions: no triplet-parallel slogans, no "It's not X. It's Y." framing, no "already has / already operates" filler. Save catchphrase constructions for when a catchphrase is asked for, not decks.
- Phrases that read as machine-written, flagged by the consultant on the Vanguard Standard scaffold (2026-09-13), are out on every paper: "the chain cannot guess", "what a client keeps using", "it already has", "after the deck has been read", and any title that personifies a tool or workflow as knowing, guessing or deciding. Test: would a consultant write this sentence in an email to the CEO? If not, rewrite it as a plain statement of what the reader gets or does.
- Process notes are not titles. Instructions the consultant gives the builder ("the section ends there", "held until tested", "write this page last") describe the build and never appear on a slide. If a note has to reach the reader, it becomes a status marker (IN DEVELOPMENT) or a footnote, not a title clause.
- No em dashes (—) in original prose: use commas, periods, or restructure. Verbatim quoted material (question text, quotes) keeps its own punctuation.
- Parallel structure within any list or numbered series: same grammatical shape, same level of specificity.

## Claims precedent (client paper)

Before drafting on client paper, skim the attached shipped deck for how far claims go (does this client accept "material risk to 2027 revenue"? Do they hedge everything?). Match that level. Any escalation beyond precedent gets flagged to the user in the draft, marked for account-partner sign-off. HOW to quantify is craft; WHETHER to introduce a new claim type is precedent.


## Edit patterns from shipped decks (Learn pass, 2026-09-12: public sector findings v10 to v13_1)

Thirteen of 33 titles and 26 of 33 pages changed between the AI draft and the shipped file. The repeated edits, in order of frequency:

- **Titles state the finding, not the method or the label.** "Secondary analysis of consulting firm articles reveal whitespace for EHR…" became "On competitor websites, EHR is scarcely covered by any of the six firms, and financial management sits at low coverage". Name where the reader would look and what they would find.
- **Claims are sized down where the number is loose.** "twice the prime-bid path" became "larger than the prime-bid path"; "no leader reported a negative experience with Huron" became "Huron's three mentions are too few to read as a reputation". A ratio survives only when it is exact.
- **Fewer names in a title.** Four firms became the two that matter, with the agencies where they matter: "Booz Allen and Leidos hold the largest positions at VA and DHA".
- **Fiscal-year ranges are written "FY24-25"**, never "FY24 to FY25" (five instances).
- **Numerals from 10 upward.** "Seventeen of the thirty-five markets" became "17 out of 35 markets"; "THE SEVENTEEN, LARGEST FIRST" became "THE 17 LARGEST". Below ten, words ("eight content topics").
- **One source line per deck, without internal counts.** "…ten recent former federal executives; 310 coded quotes." lost the quote count on every page that used it. Repository or dashboard names ("Federal Public Sector Quote Repository", "Dashboard v27") are replaced by what the source is.
- **Method caveats leave client-facing pages.** The sub-share convention, uncoded interviews, "blank cells mark a stage where…", the phase-2 scope note and the vehicles gate were cut from the open-questions and body pages. They belong in methodology or the appendix, or nowhere.
- **A page that argues one number shows it large.** The AI page lost its three-column "where Huron could sit" grid and gained a 32pt stat ("14%", "of AI market held by Accenture Federal") and a "Buyers say:" quote. Density down, point up.
- **Recommendation replaces Takeaway on finding pages**, and it says what to do: "Recommendation: Huron's page appears well on consulting-intent search. Improve AI visibility over time by publishing topical, named pieces."
- **Recommendation sentences name the client's priorities and carry the imperative in "should", not in a command verb.** "Run CMS, VHA, and DHA as named accounts and lead with digital and program credentials" became "CMS, VHA, and DHA are priority accounts that should be pursued with digital and program credentials". Jargon nouns (named accounts, prime-bid shortlist) go.
- **How-to matrices use parallel imperative cells.** "Capability briefings they request, before contracting is involved" became "Hold capability briefings"; row numbers dropped; empty cells read "N/A"; column heads became "How to reach" and "How to compete".
- **Symbols are explained in words on the page.** "▲ fastest growing" legends became "Triangle: one of the three fastest-growing best-fit markets."
- **Quote hygiene.** Straight quote marks became curly on every quote; a pronoun with no antecedent inside a quote got a bracketed referent ("[AI tools] are not being used…"); an attribution spells the agency out ("Former Market Lead, Defense Health Agency").
- **Word list confirmed.** "carry" was edited out of a title ("Three campaigns carry what buyers said…" became "…that reflect what buyers want to hear").
- **Format.** Body text on rebuilt pages moved from 10 and 11pt to 12pt; multi-colour chevrons became one accent colour; icons and thin divider lines were added to rebuilt concept pages; the 9pt chart category labels on Huron paper were left as they were.

## Two rules from the EDR AI minipack revise (2026-09-12)

- **A negative finding carries its opportunity.** When the data says the client is behind, not considered, or at risk, the same page says what the good news is or what would improve it, from the data or from what the client already does well. "Only 26% would consider Huron for digital projects" travels with "82% of its clients rate its quality 8 or higher, and its data modernization and security work is the groundwork AI needs." A finding that is only bad news reads as a verdict, not a brief.
- **Burning-platform tone is used sparingly.** Revenue-at-risk and cost-of-waiting framings are legitimate once per deck, sized to the evidence, and never as the register of every page. The consultant chooses the strength; offer two, one at the partner's strength and one sized to the data, and apply the one chosen.


## Edit patterns from shipped decks (Learn pass 2, 2026-09-12: EDR AI minipack, AI draft v3 to consultant edit)

The consultant kept the storyline, the four findings and every recommendation band, and rebuilt the pages around them. 9 of 11 body pages changed; 2 pages were removed, 2 added, 3 brought back from the appendix.

- **Cover title names the topic and the stake, not the sentence.** "AI is outpacing governance; buyers expect fees to change" became "The State of AI in Higher Ed: Risks and Opportunities for Huron". The one-sentence takeaway moved to its own callout page right after the cover (large type on the "Callout Option 1" layout, about 30 words): "AI displacement risk is real and named by higher ed buyers. Huron is poised to be a thought leader in the space of responsible AI that understands what's at stake in higher ed." Earlier rule revised: on Huron paper the cover carries topic and stake, the callout page carries the takeaway.
- **The interpretive colon clause leaves the title and goes to the band.** "…and put data privacy first among AI risks: the opening is governance, not the case for AI" lost its clause; the band took the thought. Where a second clause stays, it states what it means for the client: "…Huron digital is well regarded for those who used it, so perception is the missing link".
- **Finding lines name the client's opportunity.** "Leaders rank AI governance second…" became "Leaders need help with AI governance and data privacy risks"; bodies end "Huron is well positioned to answer their needs". A finding written as a need the client can meet outranks one written as a ranking.
- **Recommendation bands: bold the two or three phrases that carry the action, add the client proof the consultant knows, cut meta lines.** "…field-tested in large hospitals and universities" was added; "The wider AI report is where that association gets built" was cut. Bands end on the client's position ("Huron should be the voice of reason: it understands the risks involved").
- **Bold the key phrase and the number inside callout sentences**, aubergine. Grey chart subtitles are accepted.
- **Competitor charts carry logos, not firm names**, and the client's row sits in a dashed outline box.
- **Cross-survey evidence is welcome when labelled.** Quotes from a sister survey sit beside the stats under a head that names them ("On a separate healthcare survey, leaders answered…") and a footnote with question and n.
- **Page patterns the consultant chose for a minipack** (each finding may run two pages sharing the eyebrow number): a stat column with three or four large percentages beside quote cards; two ranked bar charts side by side; a small-multiple matrix of attributes by segment with coloured header chips; a quotes page on the side-panel layout in two themed groups; a readiness table with icons and a key-takeaways panel.
- **The conclusion and discussion page was dropped.** The minipack closes on next steps; discussion prompts live in the bands. The six-headlines page went to the appendix.
- **The side-panel layout ("1_Content Header Only") is for low-text pages**, to vary the rhythm: exec summary, quote pages, next steps. Finding pages with exhibits stay on the white layout.
- **Next steps page:** numbered aubergine squares, a bold lead phrase per step, owners bold in aubergine (client) or teal (Telesto), generous row spacing, no subtitle above the table.
- **Chart subtitles shortened to one line** ("Familiarity and consideration for digital projects").
- **Word budget observed on the shipped-ready pages:** title, body and chart labels together ran 120 to 260 words per body page, footnotes apart; the one page above 300 drew the comment "wordy".


## Edit patterns from shipped decks (Learn pass 3, 2026-09-13: EDR AI minipack v6 to the consultant's final)

- **One implication band per page, not one per stat.** Seven "For Huron:" lines under seven stats (asked for by comment, built as asked) were replaced by a single band at the foot of the page headed "What this means for Huron:". The register label may vary by page: "Recommendation:" where the page tells the client what to do, "What this means for Huron:" where it reads out an implication. The consultant's own verdict: the per-stat pattern was not the right call even though it was requested; when a comment asks for something that will crowd the page, propose the single band as the alternative before building.
- **Titles state the client's strategic reading, not the survey structure.** "Leaders agree on the AI risks that matter…; they differ by institution type, function and seniority" became "Higher ed leaders agree that digital and AI is their critical path towards financial and strategic leadership, but adoption and comfort level remains uneven".
- **Exec-summary bodies end on the client's position**, in the client's words: "…which is where Huron can highlight its track record"; "leadership in AI capabilities has become crucial". Finding 4 kept both facts (R1s and AMCs furthest ahead) rather than choosing one, and grew a second sentence on what institutions already do with AI.
- **Footnotes are dark grey (5A5A5A / 7F7F7F on Huron paper), not ink.** An accepted exception to the grey-text rule for the footnote home only.
- **Next steps soften to "Discuss and select…"** and the theme list was cut back to "themes that will lead into 2027"; naming the themes was left to the room.
- **Softer verbs in segment sentences:** "does not see it deployed actively" for "has the least of it deployed".
- **Recommendation bands may point to a Huron product where one exists:** "a branded Huron product should lead the conversation" replaced "should lead the service conversation". The consultant knows the offer; the draft names the service and leaves the product call to them.

## Learn pass 4 (2026-09-13, EDR AI minipack v4 edit)

- When two survey findings in a section say the same thing, the consultant replaced one with a finding from another source in the pack (the media scan) that spoke to the same audience. Check the appendix for a finding before letting two summary points overlap.
- Two pages may share an eyebrow in any section, not only Part 2.
- Section summary titles read as the section's question ("What the findings mean for Huron's AI report and marketing campaign", "Implications for Huron's digital and AI business"); the "Part 1 / Part 2" label is not carried on the slide.
- Segment lines end in what the segment will want to see, in plain words, with less data than the draft carried.

## Lessons from the first live Revise (moved from SKILL.md 2026-09-13, text unchanged) (EDR AI minipack, 2026-09-12)

- **Use the client's plain layouts.** On Huron paper the team uses two or three: the cover ("Cover Option 1"), the white content page ("Content Header Only", title across the top, footer and page number placeholders), and the divider ("Divider Option 1"). Layouts whose name starts with a number ("1_Content Header Only", "3_…", "4_…") carry a coloured side panel and are for select pages only; a finding page never goes on one. Take footer and page-number placeholders from the layout, never by copying them from another slide.
- **Look at the page before handing it over, cheaply.** Read the contact sheets `render_check.py` produces (nine pages per image), not one image per page; on an iteration render only the changed pages. Iterations patch the saved file with a short script; the full build script is written once. (Token rules in deck-qc, Token economy.)
- **Look at the page before handing it over.** Estimates of overflow and overlap from the XML are not enough. On Windows, `deck-qc/scripts/render_check.py` exports each body page to PNG through PowerPoint and lists every text frame whose text is taller than its box; look at the PNGs. Elsewhere, soffice and pdftoppm. A page is not built until it has been seen.
- **One implication band per page.** Never an implication line under every stat or bullet, even when a comment asks for it; propose the single closing band ("Recommendation:" or "What this means for [client]:") as the alternative and build that on approval.
- **The eyebrow number is a finding number, not a page number.** Two pages that both support finding 1 both carry "1". Never renumber to make pages consecutive.
- **Chart attribute labels are black.** The category label column, the data labels and the segment headers are ink; aubergine and the client accent are for the highlighted bar and for bold lead-ins only.
- **Do not write "the good news" or "the bad news".** State the positive fact under a neutral head ("Where Huron already stands") and let the number carry it.
- **Appendix and data pages stay as they are.** Crosstabs, backup exhibits and methodology pages are not restyled, resized or re-fonted by the AI phase or by the gate; smaller type is allowed there. Only hygiene (author, notes, metadata) applies to the whole file.
- **When a text run is reused, reset its formatting.** A first run inherited from an old paragraph can carry a hyperlink, an underline or a colour; strip them and set colour, size and weight explicitly, or the new text comes out pink and underlined.
